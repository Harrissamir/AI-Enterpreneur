# India's Labour Codes — what changed (status as checked in 2026)

## Status
All four Labour Codes came into force on 21 November 2025: the Code on Wages 2019, the Industrial Relations Code 2020, the Code on Social Security 2020, and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions (OSH) Code 2020. Central rules followed at the end of December 2025. They replaced 29 older central labour laws (including the Payment of Wages Act, Minimum Wages Act, Industrial Disputes Act, Factories Act, Payment of Gratuity Act, EPF Act and ESI Act — the obligations now sit under the Codes).

Important caveat: states notify their own rules, and as of mid-2026 several states had not notified all of them. What applies to a specific office depends on the state. Always check the state position before acting.

## Changes employers most often ask about
- **50% wage rule:** basic + DA + retaining allowance should be at least 50% of total remuneration; excluded allowances above the limit count as wages for PF and gratuity.
- **Gratuity for fixed-term employees:** payable after one year of continuous service (pro-rata). Permanent employees keep the five-year threshold.
- **Full and final settlement:** wages due on separation must be paid within two working days.
- **Working hours:** up to 48 hours a week; overtime at twice the ordinary rate. Daily limits and four-day-week patterns depend on the rules applicable to the establishment.
- **Standing orders and retrenchment approval thresholds:** raised from 100 to 300 workers. Smaller establishments still owe notice and compensation.
- **Gig and platform workers:** a social security fund financed by aggregator contributions.
- **Appointment letters:** expected for every employee.

These summaries are general information. The precise duty depends on state rules, headcount and category of employee.
