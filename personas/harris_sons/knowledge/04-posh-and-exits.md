# PoSH compliance and exits

## PoSH (Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace Act, 2013)
- Every workplace with 10 or more employees must constitute an Internal Committee (IC): a senior woman employee as Presiding Officer, at least two employee members, and one external member from an NGO or association committed to the cause of women, or a person familiar with sexual-harassment issues. At least half the members must be women.
- Employers must have a written PoSH policy, display the consequences of harassment and IC details, run awareness programmes, and file an annual report (to the District Officer, and in the company's annual report where applicable).
- The obligation applies even to small Indian teams of a foreign company once the threshold is met; remote and hybrid work does not remove it.
- Non-compliance can lead to fines, and repeated violations to higher penalties and possible cancellation of licences or registrations.

## Exits and terminations
- Follow the contract's notice clause and the applicable standing orders or state rules.
- Misconduct-based termination needs a fair process: written charge, opportunity to respond, and a documented inquiry proportionate to the allegation.
- Final settlement within two working days of separation under the Code on Wages; gratuity where eligible; relieving and experience letters are expected by Indian employees.
- Mutual separation agreements with clear release terms reduce dispute risk.
- Termination of a woman during maternity benefit, or as retaliation for a PoSH complaint, carries serious legal risk.

Every exit should be reviewed case by case. This is general information, not advice on a specific matter.
