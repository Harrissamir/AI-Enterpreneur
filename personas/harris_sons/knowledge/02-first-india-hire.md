# Hiring your first people in India — the decisions that come first

## 1. How will you employ them? (the most important early decision)
- **Employer of Record (EOR):** a third party legally employs the person on your behalf and runs payroll, PF, tax and statutory compliance. Fastest (days to weeks), no Indian entity needed, good for 1–10 hires. Monthly per-head fee; you control day-to-day work but the EOR is the legal employer.
- **Own Indian entity (usually a Private Limited subsidiary):** full control and lower per-head cost at scale, but incorporation, registrations, accounting and ongoing filings take months and fixed cost. Usually sensible once headcount and commitment are clear.
- **Independent contractors:** quick and cheap on paper, but the highest misclassification risk if the person works like an employee (fixed hours, your tools, ongoing integrated work, exclusivity, supervision). Misclassification can create back-liability for social security contributions, gratuity and tax, and permanent-establishment questions for the foreign company.

The right answer depends on headcount plan, timeline, budget, and how long you intend to stay. Harris & Sons helps choose and then sets it up properly.

## 2. Define the role before sourcing
Write down: outcomes for the first 6 months, seniority, must-have vs nice-to-have skills, reporting line, time-zone overlap needed, budget range in INR (not converted EUR guesses), and interview panel. Most failed India hires trace back to vague scoping or a salary band set from a different market.

## 3. Compensation basics that surprise foreign employers
- Indian offers are usually expressed as annual CTC (cost to company), which bundles fixed pay, allowances, employer PF contribution and sometimes gratuity and bonus. Candidates compare CTC, so be explicit about fixed vs variable.
- Under the Code on Wages, basic pay + dearness allowance + retaining allowance should be at least 50% of total remuneration; excess excluded allowances are added back as "wages" for PF and gratuity calculations. Old salary structures with a low basic may need redesign.
- Notice periods of 30–90 days are common, so senior candidates may take 2–3 months to join; buy-outs are negotiated.
- Counter-offers from current employers are frequent. Plan for them.

## 4. Paperwork before day one
Written appointment letter, employment contract with confidentiality and IP assignment, background verification, statutory registrations (via EOR or your entity), payroll and tax set-up, PoSH policy and Internal Committee where applicable.

## 5. Typical timeline
Role scoping 1 week → sourcing and screening 2–4 weeks → offer → notice period 30–90 days. With an EOR, compliance set-up can run in parallel.

These are general points, not legal advice for a specific situation. Specifics depend on state, headcount and the employment model — book a call to scope your case.
